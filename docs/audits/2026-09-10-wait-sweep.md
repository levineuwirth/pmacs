# The wait sweep (2026-09-10, E5.0)

One audit of every wait, poll loop, deadline, fixed sleep and read
timeout in `tests/`, `tests/common/` and the `#[cfg(test)]` modules
under `src/` and `pmacs-gpu/src/`, asking of each what its bound
actually bounds against what its caller believes it bounds. Dated and
never required reading (D4's shape); the row-level tables below are the
record, this header is what was concluded from them, and the phase's
handoff pass in the vault argues over both.

## Why

Five phases of review found one product defect and a lengthening
catalogue of witnesses that could not say what they measured: a 2 s
pump deadline live as R5 and #263, a fixture whose real constraint was a
500 ms listener window under a 2 s deadline (#264), a perf gate whose
1100 per-operation timeouts admitted 91 minutes with nothing printed
(#268), a readiness probe that discarded the `Hello` it read and so
declared a booting daemon ready (the `read Hello` family, fifteen CI
occurrences, #258), and a PTY fixture budget calibrated inside the
platform's own distribution (#259, seven occurrences).

Two rules were applied throughout. **D12**: a wall-clock number belongs
under `--perf`, where a red is a measurement, and not in the default
sweep, where it is a gate; a deadline asserts that something eventually
happens and is widened where it is tight. **The cadence fact** (run
34484377105): a `thread::sleep(20 ms)` returns at a mean of 78--94 ms on
the hosted macOS runners against 20.07 ms on Linux, a `sleep(2 ms)` at
12--17 ms, so every millisecond deadline buys roughly a fifth of the
polls its author assumed there, and any assertion about poll counts is
suspect by construction.

## Method and its limit

The corpus was partitioned seven ways by file and read in parallel
against one rubric (a site is any wait, loop, drain, bare sleep, read
timeout or `recv_timeout`; timeouts that are the subject under test are
listed separately as "subject, not instrument"; identical uses of one
helper with one deadline may collapse into one row with the line list;
every distinct deadline value or shape is its own row). Each row carries
the site, its shape, its bound and whether the bound is per-probe,
per-operation or whole-wait, whether it runs in the default sweep or
under `#[ignore]`, what the bound really bounds, what the caller
believes, the cadence exposure, one of three dispositions, and a note.

**Limit.** The partition tables are the readers' own records; the
dispositions acted on in this phase are the non-green rows, every one of
which was read again by the session before the fix list below was cut.
The green rows ("correct as written") were not re-read individually and
stand as the partition reader's judgment. Line numbers are as of
`b15d935` and drift with any edit; the site descriptions are what
identify a row.

## What was landed here

Instrument-class and small, in this phase's branch, each with its own
witness where a witness could be written without a slow platform:

- **`tests/common/ready.rs`, `wait_for_daemon`: readiness is a served
  `Hello`.** The probe returned `Ready` on a successful connect and read
  the daemon's `Hello` under a 500 ms timeout only to keep the daemon's
  first send from logging, discarding the result. The daemon binds
  before it can serve, and on the macOS luajit leg bind-to-serving is
  p50 597 ms with a 1.1 s tail, so a test that then read its own `Hello`
  under 200--250 ms was bounded by the remainder of the boot. Now a
  connect with no `Hello` inside the window is `Pending` and the wait
  continues; the probe that first sees a `Hello` returns, and every
  later connection finds a daemon that has served one. A daemon that
  exits before serving is reported on the probe that observes it, not
  after the whole deadline (the helper's doc promised that and the
  code did not do it). Two unit rows in the helper's own module
  witness both halves and compile into every integration target.
  Bears on: the `read Hello` family (every member spawns through
  `TestDaemon`, so every member's `Hello` read now follows a served
  one), #258 (`a16_26`, the same mechanism), and `wait_for_daemon`'s
  own discarded read, the largest controllable cost on the luajit
  legs (43--47 % of elapsed). Under the rerun rule nothing here
  retires a row; a mechanism is removed and the next trunk runs are
  the evidence. CORRECTED at fix round 1 (2026-09-11): the boot was
  one mechanism and not the only one. A live daemon accepts a fresh
  connection on its accept loop's next poll --- `accept_loop` runs on
  its own thread and sleeps `ACCEPT_POLL_INTERVAL` (50 ms,
  `src/daemon.rs`) on every `WouldBlock` of its non-blocking listener,
  and the per-attach thread it spawns writes the `Hello` first; no
  dispatcher is in that path, and the registry had measured exactly
  this at C1 (connect-to-`Hello` p50 50.14 ms on Linux, "one whole
  accept quantum"). The quantum is production and persists, so the
  family does not close on a mechanism's absence: it closes when every
  reader of a fresh `Hello` has a bound the quantum's macOS tail cannot
  exhaust. Eight readers had sub-second bounds --- `statusline_segments`
  (200 ms), `theme_faces` (250 ms, twice), `vterm_stage3` (2 s), moved
  at `66195b5`; `gpu_font:234` (250 ms), `m11_5:258` and `:307` (250
  ms), `m5_5:2336` (500 ms), moved at fix round 1 --- and all eight now
  read under `ready::DEADLINE`. Every other `Hello` read in the tree is
  at 5 s or at `DEADLINE` (a grep over `: Hello = read_message` and
  `read_message::<Hello>` with the read timeout in force, at fix round
  1: zero under 5 s), and 5 s is two orders past the quantum.
- **`src/daemon_attach.rs`, #264: the fixture holds its listener until
  the caller has returned**, through a channel, instead of for a fixed
  500 ms. The real window was `[100, 600)` ms after the spawn under a 2 s
  deadline that could only ever report the wrong bound; now the window
  is the caller's return. Mechanism removed.
- **`tests/m5_perf_acceptance.rs`, #268: a whole-run ceiling of five
  minutes, a 250 ms ceiling on every drain, and a progress line every
  hundred keys.** The per-key 5 s stays; 1100 of them no longer admit
  91 minutes of silence, and a hung attach now panics naming the key it
  hung on and the samples so far. This closes #268's second candidate
  mechanism (the witness that could not bound itself) and leaves the
  first (a runner that stopped reporting) where it was.
- **Deadlines at 2 s or under in the default sweep, on waits that
  assert eventuality, widened to 10 s** (the value of `ready::DEADLINE`,
  "generous on purpose"): `src/editor.rs`'s `pump_async` (R5's site),
  `src/async_runtime.rs`'s `pump_until` and its seven hand-rolled copies
  at 500 ms, 2 s and 3 s (#263's sites), `tests/m8_1_acceptance.rs`'s
  and `tests/m4_acceptance.rs`'s 2 s pumps. Every message still carries
  the elapsed time and the poll count, so R5's discriminator (about
  2000 polls in 2 s means the pump ran and nothing settled; few means
  the pump was starved) now reads as a rate, polls per second of
  elapsed, and a red there is five times less likely to be the
  scheduler. This diagnoses nothing about #263.
- **#259's 5 s budget on the PTY child's readiness file is
  `ready::DEADLINE`** (10 s), and its sibling wait on the input file
  with it. The four green macOS samples used 1.38--4.24 s and the seven
  reds 5.00--5.08 s, so this is a margin choice under D12 and not a fix:
  the `python3` interpreter start on a hosted macOS runner is what is
  slow, the test asserts nothing about its speed, and the measurement
  on the issue stands. If the tail crosses 10 s the row fires again with
  its poll count and the choice is revisited with one more sample.

Not landed, by rule: everything larger than a line or a fixture's hold,
which is the scheduled list below.

### Summary

36 rows covering roughly 130 call sites across the three suites (the 5 s handshake timeout alone is 20 explicit sites plus 42 `attach_multi` calls, and `TestDaemon::spawn*` is 42 calls onto the one 10 s `wait_for_daemon`); 26 correct as written, 9 needs re-bounding, 1 needs a measurement first. The three most consequential: (1) m5_5:59 `reattach_when_slot_clears` returns `Ready` on any `InstanceMessage`, and a too-early v0.1 attach is answered with a readable `Goodbye(AlreadyAttached)` (m5_5:729--731), so the wait bounds "the daemon answered" rather than "the slot cleared", and its two callers (tests 3 and 4) assert only pid and liveness afterwards --- both pass with the reattach rejected; (2) m5_5:1290--1313 starts its timer after `do_handshake`, which never reads the attach frame (88--98, contrast 152--155), so the latency-injection test times the initial full-grid `CellDelta`, whose 200 ms delay began before `start`, and never the key's frame it says it verifies --- a >= 50 ms stall between lines 1277 and 1290 fails it spuriously, and the read loop has no aggregate bound; (3) m5_5:323--342 (SIGTERM) has no aggregate bound in precisely the failure mode it tests --- a daemon that ignores SIGTERM keeps the loop alive with `Ok` frames under a per-read 3 s timeout and then `Child::wait()` blocks forever, so the test hangs instead of failing. Close behind: m5_5:2336 is the only `Hello` read in the file under 500 ms rather than 5 s, bounded by the daemon's boot-after-listen (`wait_for_daemon` discards its own `Hello` read), and m9_1:694's "room to restart" drain returns at the first `Stopped`, giving its negative assertions no window after the 50 ms backoff. The Q8 30 ms jitter floor (m10_11:1288) is the one site whose right bound is not decidable from the code: it needs the daemon's un-jittered tick latency measured on the slowest sweep host. m10_11's default-sweep helpers (`pump_until`, `read_one_crdt_op`, `pump_both_until_equal`) are the cleanest waits in the three files: remaining-time-clamped read timeouts, no overrun, observed state in every failure message.

### Summary

Sixty-eight rows (collapsing identical uses) over ten suites: 56 correct as written, 10 needs re-bounding, 2 needs a measurement first. Every m9 helper is a hand-rolled 15 ms poll under a wall-clock deadline (5 s, 2 s, 1.5 s, 500 ms) with no elapsed/last-state report, and none of them is cadence-bound except the 1.5 s `list_changed` chains in m9_6/m9_7; m6_perf is entirely `#[ignore]`. The three most consequential findings: (1) gpu_invocation:1030 asserts "failed bootstrap must close its socket" with `read_message(..).is_err()` under a 5 s read timeout, so a daemon that leaves the socket open passes after 4 x 5 s — the bound bounds a timeout, the caller reads it as EOF; (2) m9_7:1003 pumps on a predicate that is already true, so the "no new buffer on re-invoke" counts run before the second invoke's round trip can create one — a zero-width negative window that cannot fail; (3) gpu_invocation has four unbounded waits in the default sweep — `daemon.wait()` at :1412 and the deadline-less broadcast loops at :895, :1076, :1088 (each read is 5 s but every unrelated `CellDelta` resets it) plus the `accept()`-blocked fixture join at :1447 — which hang rather than report. Also of note: vterm_stage2:828's partial-write witness needs a 20 ms probe to land in a 100 ms window, which on hosted macOS is 0-1 probes, so the test passes without exercising the branch it exists for; the declared 1 s sleep at m9_2:547 is a window for the fake's 250 ms delayed reply plus pipe and stdin-writer hops to reach the supervisor queue before a single `sup.tick()`, and is followed by a positive assertion so a short window fails loudly.

### Summary

Seventy-nine rows cover the 38 files (helpers counted once, identical uses collapsed with their line lists): 75 carry a disposition — 53 correct as written, 18 needs re-bounding, 4 needs a measurement first — plus two helper rows (`tick_for`, `drain_kinds`) dispositioned through their callers and two files (`m8_9`, `m10_10_perf`) with no wait site. The three most consequential findings. (1) `common/ready.rs:214-217` returns `Probe::Pending` when the daemon child has already exited, so every `TestDaemon::spawn` in the workspace waits the full 10 s before reporting a daemon that died at startup — directly contradicting `common/daemon.rs:211-213`, which promises the report "at once"; one arm change fixes every daemon suite. (2) The perf pair `m5_perf_acceptance.rs:155/:217` and `m10_11_perf.rs:74/:104-127` have no aggregate bound (1100 × `PER_KEY_TIMEOUT` = 91 min, #268) and drain with a 1 ms read timeout that `transport.rs:107-116` turns into a framing desync whenever it fires inside a frame — in m10_11 the very first drain runs while the attach-time CRDT snapshot may still be arriving, so a slow runner can desync `stream_b` before the first sample and then burn the full per-key timeouts on garbage. (3) Two default-sweep rows bound a different process's timeline with a bare sleep and fail with the wrong message when it slips: `m5_8_acceptance.rs:539` sleeps 400 ms for `pmacs --attach` to reach its countdown before injecting Ctrl-C (a late startup turns the cancel into a keystroke and the test fails at :544 as "should exit within 5s"), and `m6_8_multi_repl_acceptance.rs:659` sleeps 500 ms for three REPL children before asserting one tick drained all three; the PTY fixture's `output()` and the supervisor are the readiness signals to wait on instead. Runner-up: the 10 s-read-timeout-inside-a-20 s-loop shape in `discovery_stage2_acceptance.rs:488/:540` and `gui_stage1a_wire_acceptance.rs:48/:74`, whose 20 s deadline is unreachable on a silent stream and whose failure reads as a transport fault; and `m11_5_semantic_acceptance.rs:277` computing `by` before a 2 s drain, so its "5 s" window is 3 s and the test always spends ≈8 s.

### Summary

62 individual sites collapsed into 40 rows across 16 of the 22 files (six have none): 30 rows correct as written, 8 needs re-bounding, 2 needs a measurement first; the six budget-shaped rows are counted among the correct ones and every asserting perf twin is `#[ignore]`, out of the default sweep. The three most consequential findings. (1) Registry R7 (pmacs-gpu/src/attach.rs:1953) cannot be its 1 s deadline: that deadline is armed after the spawn and consulted only after two synthetic, immediate failures, while the `Hello` read and `server.join()` that actually wait carry no bound, so the row's next failure can only be a hang rather than a red; the sibling test at 2021 uses its 300 ms startup timeout as a disguised readiness window for a child shell to write its stderr line. (2) Registry R5 (src/editor.rs:13190) bounds a first `tick` whose cost is proportional to a backlog the test never bounds — `emit_n(1_000_000)` sends one envelope per item on an unbounded bus until the worker sees a cancel flipped later in the same Lua eval, and `tick` drains the whole backlog in one call — so a bigger `DEADLINE` is not the fix until the backlog at cancel is measured on the macOS runner. (3) Two tests believe libtest has a per-test timeout and rely on it to bound an unbounded `Drop` join (src/worker.rs:559, 588); it has none, so a leaked worker hangs the sweep, and src/lua_isolation.rs:304/342 have the same shape (an infinite Lua loop with no bound on its `exec`). Issue #264 (src/daemon_attach.rs:846) and registry U19 (src/daemon.rs:5294) are confirmed as stated: the former's 2 s deadline outlives the fixture's 500 ms hold by 1.4 s so the `elapsed < 2 s` assertion names the wrong window, and the latter's 5 s bound on a PTY child's first byte reports neither `bell_count` nor elapsed, which is what makes its row unreadable.
