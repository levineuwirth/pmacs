# The wall-clock classification (2026-10-12, E0.6 continued)

One classification of every assertion in the default test sweep that
reads a clock, measured under five conditions, by E0.6's method and the
verdicts it set. Dated and never required reading (D4's shape); the
row-level table below is the record, this header is what was concluded
from it, and the pass's Passus in the vault argues over both. It changes
no code: no `#[ignore]` was added, no bound widened, no row split,
nothing added to `scripts/perf-budgets` and nothing retired. Applying
the verdicts is a later phase's work.

## Why

E0.6 took the wall-clock budgets out of the default run so that a loaded
machine could not fail a sweep on speed, and left the deadlines in it
under a rule: a deadline is widened where its bound is under ten times
the observed value. Fourteen phases have added rows since, and the
intermittent reds they produce have been diagnosed one at a time. This
pass re-derives the whole set on one tree, measures each site quiet and
under CPU and disk load, and gives each a verdict with its reason, so
that the next phase moves rows from a list rather than from a hunch.

## The set, and the two instruments that found it

**Definition.** A site is in the set when a check that fails the test reads a wall-clock value: an assert-family macro whose condition reads one, or an `if` on one whose block panics, asserts or returns an `Err` the caller fails on. The value may be an `Instant`, a `Duration` from `.elapsed()` or `duration_since`, a duration a helper or closure returns, or a time the product reports (`duration_ms`, `after`, `age_ms`, `cursor_ms`). By that definition the readiness waits that assert `Instant::now() < deadline` in their own loop are in, and so are `tick_until`, `pump_until`, `pump_async` and `collect_matches`: wc3 included those four only because they spell the check `elapsed < deadline`. Out, and counted: waits that end on the clock and leave the caller to assert the condition (`assert!(pump_until(...))`), `recv_timeout(...).expect`, fixed observation windows, and two deadlines in product code that are the subject of their tests (`pmacs-gpu/src/attach.rs:924`, `src/daemon_attach.rs:283`).

**wc3** (the first instrument, an `.elapsed()`-binding scan): 51 rows on `824ca82`, of which 33 are real, 15 false, 2 helpers no default row calls, and 1 an `#[ignore]` row. Its errors have three shapes. It collects bound names file-wide, so `s`, `row`, `md`, `notes` bound from `.elapsed()` in one function flag unrelated asserts: eight of its nine `e7h_grammar_gate_acceptance` rows, four `e7_format_on_save` lines and three `async_runtime` lines. Its attribute scan stops at a multi-line `#[allow(…)]`, so two `#[ignore]` measurements read as default-sweep helpers. And it reads only `src/` and `tests/`, and only names bound from an `.elapsed()` on the right-hand side. That misses `pmacs-parse-unit/tests/worker.rs:141`, every `took = save(&s)`, the closure-returned `fast` in `m5_8` that E0.6 itself classified, `max_tick`, and the `*_ms` fields.

**Mine** (`wcb.py`): every workspace crate, comments and strings masked, bindings propagated to a fixed point per function, plus ordering comparisons against `Duration::from_*` and named bound constants. It found every real wc3 site and the rest. Its own false positives (propagation through helpers whose signature carries a `Duration`: `events`, `evs`, `src_text`; a schedule's computed delays) were read and dismissed by hand, each named in the Passus.

**Reconciled: 172 sites run in the default sweep**, in 158 rows and helpers: 63 that measure an interval (tier A, E0.6's class) and 109 waits whose own failing check reads the clock (tier B). 51 more sit in `#[ignore]` rows. Nine helpers have no default caller, among them both `wait_warm`s (`e7b_status_acceptance`, `e7c_check_acceptance`): every caller is a `#[ignore]` measurement against rust-analyzer, so neither runs in the default sweep.

## How each site was measured

Each site was patched with one `eprintln!` before its check, printing what the check reads and its bound; the patch was generated, applied in this worktree, built in the gate's target, and reverted (`git status` empty, the gate below run on the reverted tree). Every run used the gate's environment: its target dir, the five ambient roots, a short `TMPDIR` under `~/build/pmacs-gate-targets/tmp` with `XDG_RUNTIME_DIR` in it, the sccache shim, every `PMACS_REQUIRE_*` the gate arms; each binary run directly with cargo's working directory and `--show-output`. Conditions, by the letter the table uses:

- **q**: the full instrumented sweep, quiet: median load 2.05 at each binary's start.
- **L**: the full instrumented sweep under the Epiphany rounds' fuzzers and builds, load 14 to 105. Its build printed no deadline for a wait whose bound is a parameter, so two calls with different timeouts could merge; L is not used for those sites.
- **t**: three targeted repetitions of every test with a site under ten, load 1.5 to 5.8.
- **D**: those tests and five more that save or write, three repetitions, beside a looping 2 GB `fdatasync` writer (E8c review 1's instrument).
- **W**: the full instrumented sweep beside the same writer.

A site whose check sits after its loop's success test and was never reached held at the first probe in every run; the table bounds it by its whole test's time from an uninstrumented sweep under load. The ratio is the bound over the worst observed value. Where the observed interval is mostly a delay the fixture or the product schedules on purpose (a fake server's hold, a grace, a backoff, an injected slow sync), the slack ratio, (bound − delay) / (observed − delay), is given beside it: that is how E0.6 read `daemon_attach`'s "20× and 50× margins", and for those rows it is the number that says whether load can fail them.

## Verdicts

E0.6's three (budget, deadline, mixed → split), the fourth E0 review 1's Low 1 found (**not time**: the subject is not time, and the assertion that would replace the clock is named), **floor** for a lower bound (load lengthens an interval and cannot fail a floor; the ten-times rule does not apply to it), and **insufficient evidence**. "Discriminating" marks a deadline whose ceiling separates two mechanisms, after E0.6; it cannot widen past the slower one.

Tally, by first word of the verdict: deadline 130 (of them, 6 "designed delay", 2 read on their slack, 3 helpers), deadline, discriminating 18, floor 12, not time 4, already split 3, floor + deadline 2, floor + deadline, discriminating 2, mixed 1; 172.

## What the measurements say

**Thirty-two sites fall under ten times in at least one condition, and the quiet number hides six of them.** Twenty-six are under ten quietly. Six clear ten quietly and fall under it loaded: two under CPU load, `pump_async` (`editor.rs:13516`, 76 → 7.1 on `grep_supersede_via_lua_cancels_predecessor`) and `process.rs:5201` (17 → 6.5), and four under disk load, `setsid_escapee_is_not_reaped_and_teardown_reclaims_readers` (13 → 0.84, red at 1.78 s), `e7_3_on_the_save_writes_the_formatted_text` (33 → 1.09), `e7_3_an_erroring_formatter_saves_unformatted_and_says_so` (31 → 1.51) and `m4_24_d3_no_overlap_when_a_walk_outlives_its_interval` (13 → 6.5).

**Most low ratios are not load: they are a delay the test schedules.** Twenty-two of the thirty-two observe an interval that is mostly a fake server's hold, a grace, a backoff, an injected slow sync or a fixture's sleep. Quiet and CPU-loaded agree on them to a few percent, `process.rs:5342` (0.30 against 0.43 s) aside. For those the literal ratio says nothing about load and the slack ratio does: fifteen clear ten on their slack and seven do not, five of the format-on-save family, `process.rs:5342` and the autosave strace row.

**The load the quiet number hides is the disk.** Beside the writer, the format-on-save family's ceilings fail outright: `e7_3_a_stalled_formatter…` 2.58 s against 1.3 s, `e7_3_a_late_answer_is_never_applied` 2.60 s against 0.9 s, `e7_review1_a_late_answer_past_the_default_bound…` 4.53 s against 1.2 s, `e7_review1_a_server_killed_during_the_wait…` 4.52 s against 2.5 s, where quietly they read 0.22–1.02 s. All seven of the family's checks time `save()`, which includes the buffer's own write and sync, not just the wait on the formatter that their subjects name. These reds were induced on purpose by the disk writer and are measurements, not occurrences; they move no count. Whether issue 364's 1.34 s on macOS or issue 320's 11 s locally was this mechanism is not established here.

**Four checks do not measure time, and each has a clock-free replacement:**
- `process.rs:4930` (issue 358): read `sup.reap_ledger[&pgid].deadline` before and after the second terminate and assert it unmoved; the ledger arms insert-if-absent, the unit test sits in the same module, and the SIGKILL event stays asserted.
- `daemon_attach.rs:864`: `result.is_ok()` already says the 2 s timeout was not hit.
- `e7_review1_probes.rs:330` (issue 320): the status `formatted (2 edits)` already says the answer came in time; what the clock adds is the save's disk speed.
- `e7i_review3_probes.rs:871`: `held=false` and `crashes=0`, asserted above it, already say no back-off held the parse (`held` reads `backoff_until`).

**One mixed row E0.6's list missed:** `m4_10_index_persists_across_sessions` asserts `cold-start < 2s` beside its persistence checks. It is a budget riding a correctness row; split it.

**The three verdicts this pass could not reach by measuring alone:**
- `setsid_escapee…`: its 2 s ceiling caps any widening, and whether the product records which path ended the drain is unestablished.
- `vterm_stage2`'s smoke row: it waits a constant 3.0–3.1 s of 5 s on a timer this pass did not identify.
- `e6b_4…` (`e6b_styling_under_edit_acceptance.rs:368`): discriminating against its 1.5 s whole-document hold at 9.4.
